EvidenceWhat the numbers show

These figures give a snapshot of education, work, and internet use in the Philippines, followed by selected global comparisons. Each card names the group counted and the year the data covers.

How to read these figures: A percentage means “out of 100.” NEET means a young person is not in employment, education, or training; it does not tell us whether that person is looking for a job. SEA-PLM is a Southeast Asian assessment that tests Grade 5 reading and mathematics. These figures cover different groups and years, so they are context—not a direct, like-for-like comparison.

The age groups and dates differ from card to card. Read each figure with its date and description rather than treating all of them as one ranking.

Some figures are older because they are the most recent comparable data cited by the source. Their dates are shown so they are not mistaken for current counts.

FrameworkLearn · Lead · Act

Young people need chances to practise what they learn, take responsibility, and solve real problems with others.

Learn: understand a problem and build useful skills. Lead: listen, work with others, and make decisions. Act: put those skills to use in a school, barangay, or community—and learn from the result.

The advocacyWhy education and leadership belong together

Young people need the skills and the chance to help shape the places where they live, study, and work.

Education helps young people read, question information, solve problems, and use digital tools. Those skills matter, but they do not go far if students never get the chance to use them.

Young people also need chances to speak up, make decisions, and help with issues affecting their schools and communities. Arete brings these ideas together through learning, leadership, and community action.

Making learning more accessible

Help students get learning opportunities and build the skills to ask questions, think clearly, and solve problems.

Building leadership skills

Give young people chances to communicate, work with others, make decisions, and take responsibility.

Taking part in community decisions

Make room for young people to contribute to issues affecting their schools and communities.

Why I support this advocacyWhat shaped my commitment

I learned what youth leadership can look like outside the classroom.

I became interested in youth empowerment through the Montessori Model United Nations (MMUN) and the Harvard Workshop on Innovation and Leadership (HWIL). These experiences let me hear different viewpoints, work with other students on difficult issues, and turn discussions into proposals.

What I took away was simple.

Young people do not have to wait until adulthood to contribute.

They need room to try, support when they get stuck, and people who take their ideas seriously.

What I do

I put that belief into practice by helping other students prepare, learning beyond class, and taking responsibility in student organizations.
